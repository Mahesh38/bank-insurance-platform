## 2. The five questions

### Q4 · Is recording a missing consent reference acceptable, rather than rejecting the proposal?

**Today:** a proposal submitted without `consentRef` is **accepted**, and an audit event
`CONSENT_REF_MISSING` is written. It is a soft gate.

**The alternative** is to reject the submission outright. That is currently scheduled for Phase
5.5 and would need to be pulled forward through change control.

**What Engineering can tell you:** making it mandatory is a small code change. The business
consequence is not small — an adviser mid-conversation with a customer would be blocked, and how
often that happens depends on whether the calling app reliably supplies the reference. Nobody has
measured that.

> **Your call:** ACCEPTABLE FOR UAT / MUST BE HARD-REJECTED NOW / ACCEPTABLE WITH CONDITIONS

---

### Q5 · Is the masking rule set sufficient?

**No regulation prescribes an algorithm.** IRDAI and RBI require personal data to be protected
and leave the method to the entity's policy. So this needs your judgement against the bank's
data-protection policy and the DPDP Act — not a citation.

| Field | Masked as | Rationale offered | The objection |
|---|---|---|---|
| Name `Asha` | `A***` | Initial only | — |
| Mobile `9876543210` | `******3210` | Support can confirm the right customer | 4 digits + a name + a branch can re-identify |
| Email `asha.rao@example.com` | `a***@e***.com` | Domain shape retained | — |
| PAN `ABCDE1234F` | `*****1234F` | Correlate records without exposing the holder | **The retained part is the 4-digit serial plus the check letter — narrower than it looks** |
| DOB `1990-01-15` | `****-**-**` | Removed entirely | — |

Masking applies both by field name and by pattern, so a PAN inside a free-text error message is
also masked. Real before/after output: [audit-log-samples.md](./audit-log-samples.md) —
generated by the code, not written by hand.

**Engineering's view:** the mobile rule is defensible; the **PAN rule is the one to look at
hardest**. Tightening either is a change in one class with tests, and the samples regenerate on
the next build.

> **Your call, per field:** SUFFICIENT / TIGHTEN (specify) — and in particular, is retaining the
> last 5 of a PAN acceptable?

### Q6 · What retention applies, and to what?

**This is the question with the most at stake, and Engineering's reading is that the current
figure is wrong.**

| Source | Requirement | Applies to |
|---|---|---|
| IRDAI (Maintenance of Information…) Regulations, **2025** | **10 years minimum** from last transaction | Insurance records |
| IRDAI Information & Cyber Security Guidelines, **2023** | **180 days rolling**, maintained *and monitored*, **stored in India** | ICT / application logs |
| PMLA + PML (Maintenance of Records) Rules | **5 years** from transaction date | Transaction and KYC records |

**Our current default is 7 years for raw payloads** — between the PMLA floor and the IRDAI
figure, satisfying neither cleanly. It was an engineering default, not a compliance instruction.

**The question underneath it, which Engineering cannot answer:**

> Is `audit_event` an **ICT log** (180 days) or an **insurance record** (10 years)?

Engineering suspects the answer differs by event type — business events like
`PROPOSAL_SUBMITTED` are records, transport events like `ONESB_OUTBOUND_CALL` are logs. **If that
split is right, the schema needs a column to distinguish them**, which is far cheaper to add now
than once the table has years of data in it.

Full detail and sources: [REGULATORY-RETENTION-FINDINGS.md](./REGULATORY-RETENTION-FINDINGS.md).
Those are secondary sources; the primary texts need checking by someone who can read them
authoritatively.

> **Your call:** retention period for (a) `raw_payload`, (b) `audit_event` — split by event type
> if applicable, (c) `integration_job` / offers / poll attempts, which currently have none.

### Q7 · Is best-effort capture the right trade-off?

**Provisionally accepted by the Architect**, referred to you to confirm.

Both raw-payload capture and audit persistence are **best-effort**: if the persistence service is
unavailable, the failure is logged and the customer's transaction proceeds. The evidence is lost
silently.

The alternative is to fail the customer's quote or proposal when evidence cannot be recorded.

**Engineering's view:** best-effort is right for Phase 4 — a hard dependency turns a persistence
outage into a customer-facing outage. But the honest cost is that a gap in the evidence trail is
invisible, and it is precisely during an incident that both are most likely.

Worth noting: a middle option exists that nobody has costed — a local durable queue that retries,
so evidence survives a persistence outage without blocking the customer.

> **Your call:** ACCEPTABLE / MUST BE TRANSACTIONAL / ACCEPTABLE WITH A RETRY BUFFER

---

### Q8 · Do `actor_id`, `agent_id` and `journey_id` constitute personal data?

The audit schema stores no direct identifier — no name, PAN, mobile, email or date of birth. It
does store:

| Field | What it is | Identifies |
|---|---|---|
| `actor_id` | Bank employee identifier of the person who acted | A named employee, via HR records |
| `agent_id` | Selling agent identifier | A named agent |
| `journey_id` | Correlates one customer's session end to end | A customer, when joined with other records |

Under DPDP, identifiability *by reference* generally still counts. If these are personal data,
consequences follow for retention, access control, and possibly erasure rights — noting that
erasure and an immutable audit log are in direct tension, and that tension is worth naming before
it becomes a request nobody knows how to answer.

> **Your call:** PERSONAL DATA / NOT PERSONAL DATA / PERSONAL DATA WITH EXEMPTION (specify)
